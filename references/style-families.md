# Style families

Use the relevant family section when the direction or its defining traits remain unclear. These families are not exhaustive, mutually exclusive, or fixed historical definitions.

## Use evidence from iOS apps

The following descriptions are possible visual directions, not a verified list of current iOS trends. Some families are longstanding design approaches. The schematic examples do not establish adoption in real apps.

When the user requests current iOS trends:

1. Find dated evidence about released iPhone or iPad interfaces.
2. Check actual app screens or interaction recordings.
3. Record the app, platform, source, date, and visible design trait.
4. Distinguish a recurring pattern from a single example or personal interpretation.

Prefer current App Store screenshots, developer demonstrations, and dated release notes that show the interface. Check the interface inside those sources, not the surrounding marketing page. A recent app update does not prove that its screenshots show a recent redesign.

Use Apple's current platform documentation for platform behavior and capabilities. That documentation alone does not establish adoption across independent apps.

Do not use web trend reports to substantiate iOS trends. Do not infer widespread adoption from one app. If evidence is insufficient, present the direction as a proposal rather than an established trend.

Use non-iOS references only to interpret a visual style that the user explicitly requests. Label that material as visual inspiration. Keep it separate from evidence about native iOS interfaces.

## Structural and typographic families

If a visual comparison would clarify the choice, open the [style study](examples/style-directions.png). It compares six interpretations with the same title, field, and action states. These are schematic illustrations, not implemented or tested iOS controls. The [SVG source](examples/style-directions.svg) keeps the study editable.

### Digital brutalism

Explore exposed structure, direct typography, rectangular regions, and minimal decorative depth. Let borders and spacing reveal organization. Use a deliberate grid even when the result appears raw.

Preserve legible text and clear controls. Raw appearance does not justify broken alignment or missing states. Do not automatically add bright colors or offset shadows to every brutalist interpretation.

### Neo-brutalism

Explore explicit outlines, flat fills, assertive type, and hard offset shadows. Use consistent border weight and shadow direction across related components. Define how pressing a control changes its apparent depth.

Keep hierarchy within the strong visual language. Avoid rounded glass controls appearing between angular outlined controls without a defined reason. Keep custom shadows separate from the control's hit region.

### Editorial

Explore typographic contrast, content-led spacing, fine rules, and deliberate image placement. Use system serif or sans-serif variants according to the publication's character. Define reading width and heading wraps.

Keep controls recognizable within the editorial composition. Do not turn every screen into a magazine cover. Avoid oversized titles that displace the immediate task.

### Swiss or typographic modernism

Explore a strong grid, consistent alignment, asymmetric composition, and clear type roles. Use color to organize or emphasize a small number of roles. Prefer flat surfaces when depth adds no information.

Keep the grid adaptable to large text and localization. Avoid assuming that all headings need capitals or that every surface needs a visible grid.

## Material and tactile families

### Soft tactile or neumorphic

The softer geometry in current iOS examples does not establish widespread neumorphism. Check lighting and surface behavior before applying that label.

Explore shallow raised or inset surfaces with a consistent light direction. Use soft shadows to explain elevation. Define a clear difference between passive surfaces and interactive controls.

Do not rely on shadows alone for control boundaries or selection. Preserve visible text and state indicators when contrast or transparency settings change. Avoid embedding every content group inside a soft recessed panel.

### Skeuomorphic

Choose a specific physical reference, such as a notebook, dial, or instrument. Define its materials, lighting, geometry, and state changes consistently. Use the physical metaphor where it helps the task.

Keep text adaptable instead of embedding labels into a texture. Do not require users to understand decorative physical mechanisms to find basic actions. Use supplied assets or the permitted asset workflow.

### Glass and translucent layers

Define which controls float above content and which surfaces remain opaque. Use translucency consistently within those roles. Provide readable alternatives when transparency decreases.

Generic glass styling does not make every content card an appropriate Liquid Glass surface. Avoid overlapping glass layers that obscure hierarchy.

## Expressive families

### Retro print or retrofuturist

Choose a specific reference period and medium. Define the palette, type treatment, geometry, and material cues from that choice. Distinguish printed references from imagined instruments or early digital interfaces.

Do not combine unrelated eras merely because each looks retro. Preserve the skill's restrictions on neon and generated imagery unless the user explicitly changes them. Name any adaptation that removes a defining reference trait.

### Expressive geometry or maximalism

Explore strong shape relationships, color fields, and deliberate changes in scale. Assign the strongest emphasis to the content that matters. Define quieter areas for forms, reading, and repeated actions.

Visual abundance still needs hierarchy. Avoid making every row equally loud. Do not add badges, artwork, or motion without a role in the chosen system.

### Collage or handcrafted

Explore layered crops, irregular framing, and a consistent family of permitted assets. Define where irregularity can appear and where alignment must remain stable. Keep functional controls distinct from decorative layers.

Preserve text legibility and independent hit regions. Do not generate artwork merely because the style often uses illustration. Use an asset-light interpretation when suitable source assets are unavailable.

## Add another family when the task needs it

Record defining traits, unsuitable combinations, state behavior, and applicable constraints for the new direction. Check representative references when available. Apply the same review process as for the listed families.

A small, coherent interpretation is more useful than a long list of disconnected trend effects.
