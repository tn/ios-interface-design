# Imagery

Use this reference when deciding how photos, covers, or thumbnails should fit, crop, or fail.

## Define the image's role

Identify whether the image supports recognition, comparison, inspection, or atmosphere. Set its visual emphasis according to that role. Do not enlarge an image solely to fill unused space.

For repeated image roles, define these decisions:

- The frame's aspect ratio.
- The crop or fit behavior.
- The focal point, meaning the subject that must remain visible.
- The treatment of corners, borders, and backgrounds.
- The appearance before loading and after failure.

Use the project's existing style rules for shared treatments. Keep meaningful differences between content types.

## Crop without losing meaning

Use a consistent frame for comparable items when it supports scanning. Preserve the subject when cropping. Check faces, products, embedded text, and other identifying details near the edges.

Prefer fitting the complete image when its boundaries or proportions carry information. Covers, documents, and diagrams can require this treatment. Use a suitable surrounding surface when the frame and source proportions differ.

Do not stretch images to force a common ratio. Do not use the same central crop blindly for every source. Check portrait, landscape, and unusually narrow images where the collection permits them.

Keep the selected object recognizable between a thumbnail and its detail view. A different crop can suit each context, but it should not suggest a different object.

## Keep the collection coherent

Coordinate crop behavior, framing, and visual emphasis across repeated roles. Prefer source images with compatible lighting and composition when selecting editorial assets.

Do not apply a universal filter to user photos or products merely to match the interface. Preserve colors and details that affect recognition or decisions. Do not infer exact source colors from compressed reference images.

Keep text separate from imagery where practical. If text overlays an image, check contrast across the permitted crops. Use a deliberate local surface or scrim when needed. A scrim is a layer that improves foreground contrast.

## Handle missing and changing images

Reserve the intended image area during loading. Use a neutral placeholder or a relevant symbol when an image is unavailable.

Distinguish missing content from a failed load when recovery matters. Do not insert unrelated stock photography as an error fallback. Avoid a broken-image appearance that changes row height.

Use an appropriate source resolution for the displayed size and device scale. Keep identity consistent when a sharper version replaces a preview. Do not apply a late response to a reused row containing another object.

## Check the content, not only the frame

Check representative crops at their actual display size. Include difficult source proportions and absent images. Check image descriptions when the image conveys information beyond adjacent text. Exclude purely decorative images from accessibility navigation.

Report unavailable image assets instead of claiming that placeholders represent final artwork.
